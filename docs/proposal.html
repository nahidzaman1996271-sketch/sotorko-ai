1. Problem identification and importance

Bangladesh sits on the delta of the Ganges, Brahmaputra and Meghna, and floods return every monsoon. In August 2024, flash floods in the east, described by UNICEF as the worst in three decades, hit 5.8 million people across 11 districts. More than 500,000 people crowded into 3,403 shelters, and over 2 million children were put at risk. Two years earlier, Sylhet suffered one of its worst floods in living memory.

The forecast exists. The warning does not arrive. The Flood Forecasting and Warning Centre (FFWC), the Meteorological Department (BMD) and Google Flood Hub already publish river-level and rainfall forecasts days ahead. The gap is the last mile:

  • Wrong format: warnings are technical bulletins ("danger level +45 cm at Amalshid") on websites, often in English, not instructions a farmer or a mother can act on.
  • Wrong channel: many people at risk use basic phones, have no data, or cannot read long text. Elderly and low-literacy people are left out.
  • Wrong timing: once water rises, power and towers fail (92% of Feni's towers were down at one point). A warning that leaves after the peak never arrives.
  • One-way: after a warning goes out, nobody knows who is safe, who is stranded, or which shelter is already full.
Why it matters: every hour of usable lead time lets families move people, livestock and documents to safety. The data to buy those hours already exists; what is missing is an autonomous system that acts on it for every union, every household, around the clock.

2. Proposed AI-powered solution

Sotorko AI is a team of cooperating AI agents that watches the data, decides who is at risk, and acts on its own. It alerts people in plain Bangla on whatever phone they have, routes them to a shelter that still has room, listens to their replies, and escalates the stranded to rescuers. A human officer approves every high-severity alert.

AgentWhat it does autonomously
1. MonitorPolls FFWC water levels and forecasts, BMD rainfall, Google Flood Hub and Open-Meteo every 15 minutes, and detects rising trends and threshold crossings.
2. Risk AssessorCombines forecasts, terrain and past flood extents into a risk score per union/upazila, with time-to-impact, and explains its reasoning.
3. Bangla CommunicatorWrites short, local, action-first alerts in Bangla (and Sylheti or Chittagonian phrasing where needed). Sends them by SMS, WhatsApp or automated voice call.
4. Shelter RouterFinds the nearest reachable shelter with free capacity, and redirects people when it fills up.
5. Rescue TriageReads free-text Bangla replies ("আমরা আটকে আছি, ৫ জন", "we're stuck, 5 people"), scores urgency, and dispatches volunteers or rescue teams with a location.
OrchestratorPlans and coordinates the agents, keeps an audit log, asks a human officer to approve severe alerts, and checks results: who replied "safe", who must be re-alerted.

3. How it works: workflow and user journey

One user's journey: Rahima, a mother in Fulgazi, Feni

  1. T-36 h: Monitor sees the Muhuri river forecast crossing danger level. Risk Assessor flags 14 unions in Fulgazi and Parshuram as high risk, around 30 h to impact.
  2. T-34 h: Orchestrator drafts the campaign and an Upazila officer approves it with one tap on the dashboard.
  3. T-33 h: Rahima's basic phone gets an SMS and, because she didn't open it, an automated Bangla voice call:
    সতর্কতা: আগামীকাল দুপুরের মধ্যে আপনার এলাকায় বন্যার পানি উঠতে পারে। আজই শিশু ও বয়স্কদের নিয়ে দক্ষিণ আনন্দপুর সরকারি প্রাথমিক বিদ্যালয় আশ্রয়কেন্দ্রে (১.২ কিমি) যান। নিরাপদ থাকলে "1" লিখে পাঠান, সাহায্য লাগলে "2"।
    "Warning: flood water may reach your area by tomorrow noon. Take children and elders to the South Anandapur Govt. Primary School shelter (1.2 km) today. Reply 1 if safe, 2 if you need help." The shelter name is illustrative.
  4. T-20 h: That shelter reaches 90% capacity, so Shelter Router redirects new arrivals to the next shelter that still has room.
  5. T-6 h: 18% of households haven't replied. Orchestrator re-calls them and alerts the ward volunteer and the mosque imam with the list. A reply of "2, 5 people, roof" is triaged as critical and sent to the nearest rescue boat.
  6. Peak: even when towers fail, people were warned a day earlier, and volunteers already hold offline lists of who is still at home.

4. Proposed AI approach, data and integrations

LayerTools and models
Agent reasoningAn LLM with tool calling (for example Claude) as the planner for each agent. A durable workflow engine keeps state, retries and an audit trail. Guardrails: an allow-list of tools, rate limits, and human approval above a set severity.
Flood-risk modelA gradient-boosted model (XGBoost) trained on historical FFWC water levels, BMD rainfall and past flood extents (Sentinel-1 satellite archive). Outputs a risk score and hours-to-impact per union. The LLM explains, the model decides.
Bangla languageLLM generation in Bangla with fixed templates for critical facts (time, place, shelter). Unicode normalisation (NFC), and a fine-tuned BanglaBERT classifier to triage replies (safe, stuck, medical, need boat).
VoiceBangla text-to-speech for outbound calls, and keypad or speech replies (IVR) for people who cannot read.
DeliveryBulk SMS gateway (works on every phone), WhatsApp Business API, voice-call API, and a web dashboard for officers and volunteers.
GeoUnion and upazila boundaries, OpenStreetMap roads and a shelter registry for nearest-reachable-shelter routing.

5. Target users and expected impact

Who we serve

  • Households in flood-prone unions, especially basic-phone users, the elderly, people with disabilities, and low-literacy families.
  • Union Parishad and Upazila disaster officers, who get a live risk map, one-tap approval and shelter occupancy.
  • Volunteers and NGOs (for example Red Crescent and local youth groups), who get a prioritised rescue list with locations.

Expected impact

  • More lead time: warnings go out hours to days before the peak, while networks still work.
  • Higher reach: SMS plus voice reaches people that apps and websites miss.
  • Fewer overcrowded shelters through capacity-aware routing.
  • Faster rescue: stranded families are found from their replies, not by chance.
  • Measured: reply rate, time to evacuate, unreached households, shelter balance.

6. Innovation and feasibility

What is new

  • Agentic, not a broadcast: the system plans, acts, checks results and re-acts without a human pushing buttons, while keeping a human in charge of severe decisions.
  • Two-way and closed-loop: Bangla replies become rescue tasks, and silence itself triggers escalation.
  • Capacity-aware shelter routing instead of a static "nearest shelter" list.
  • Inclusive by design: voice calls for people who cannot read, and offline volunteer lists for when towers fail.

Why it is feasible

  • The data is public: FFWC, BMD, Google Flood Hub and Open-Meteo already provide forecasts, so we build on top of them rather than replace them.
  • Proven components: LLM APIs, SMS and voice gateways, and Bangla NLP models exist today. Our work is the agent orchestration, the risk model and the Bangla user experience.
  • Phased rollout: (1) a 4-week pilot with historical data replaying the 2024 Feni flood; (2) a live pilot in one flood-prone upazila in Sylhet or Sunamganj with its Union Parishads; (3) scale through district disaster offices.
  • Risks we have planned for: false alarms (human approval, confidence thresholds, short template messages), network failure (early alerts and offline lists), and privacy (opt-in phone numbers, minimal data, encrypted storage).

7. Business model and sustainability

StreamHow it pays
Free for citizensAlerts are always free to receive. People at risk never pay.
Government (B2G)An annual subscription per district or upazila for the officer dashboard and automated alerting, through the Department of Disaster Management and local government.
NGOs and anticipatory-action fundsHumanitarian agencies already fund early action before floods. Sotorko AI provides the trigger, the reach and the audit trail they need.
Telecom partnershipMobile operators provide subsidised SMS and voice as part of their social commitments, in return for brand visibility and network-impact data.
Data servicesAnonymised, aggregated risk and evacuation insights for insurers, microfinance lenders and agriculture.

Sustainability: SMS and API costs scale with the number of people at risk, which is exactly what funders pay for. The core is open-source, so other disasters (cyclones, landslides, heatwaves) and other countries can reuse it. Local ownership through Union Parishads and volunteers keeps it working after the hackathon.
